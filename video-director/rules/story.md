# Story: how a video should be

## The spine

Every product video tells one story: **one customer → one problem → one cause → one fix → one proof**.

1. Name the customer: a fictional company or order from the buyer's world.
2. State the problem as a number that hurts: a ship date, money, a failed audit.
3. Show the cause the product reveals.
4. Do the fix in the product as one continuous action.
5. Prove it in the same view that showed the problem.

A buyer retells a story, not a feature list.

*Why:* one reviewed cut showed three problems with three different causes (an overloaded day, a job running two days late, welding steps with no welder), then proved a single fix on a wide board. Viewers could not say what was fixed or why. One concrete order has the right spine: "Cascade Bike Shop ordered 30 rear wheels … we promised delivery on September 9 … Cascade gets all 30 wheels on September 9."

## Shape of a 60 s feature video

| Beat | Seconds | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 | The problem, visible in the product (or the finished title frame, with the product on screen by ~2 s) |
| Stakes | 3–10 | One line: who is affected and what it costs |
| Cause | 10–20 | The product shows why |
| Fix | 20–40 | The action, continuous, in the product |
| Proof | 40–50 | The same view, now fixed, with a label |
| Payoff and brand | 50–60 | One line of value, lockup, URL, call to action |

Scale the beats in proportion for other lengths. The hook stays at 3 s or less at every length.

## The hook (first 3–5 s)

Open on the finished title (frame 0 doubles as the thumbnail), dock it out of the way, and state the problem as an animation before the app appears: an illustration that draws itself, a typed headline, one visible failure (a bar crossing its due date and turning red). Then cut to the app, on the music's first downbeat when possible.

## Length and pace

- Target lengths:
  - feature video: 45–75 s
  - hero teaser: 15–20 s
  - feed cut: 15–30 s
- Set timing in the edit: shorten holds and cut beats that don't serve the spine. A playback-speed change after rendering (one reviewed cut got a 1.25× pass) speeds up every ease, every cursor move and the music along with it. That cut still ran 25% over its target.
- Every shot earns its place. If removing a shot leaves the story intact, remove it.
- Every beat moves. The only still moments are reading holds sized to the words (about words ÷ 3.5 + 0.5 s). An app scene runs ~5–7 s with three or four events inside it (move, click, camera, annotation). Cut idle stretches of a recording with invisible jump cuts, and speed slow recorded actions (a drag) to 1.5–2× rather than speeding up the film.
- Frame 0 is a finished title or the product, so the first frame and the thumbnail always show something.

## Copy

- Use the product's own words (from `CONTEXT.md`) and the brand's own lines (from the brand profile).
- Non-technical buyers get plain, full sentences and no developer words.
- One headline per beat: about 10 words at most, one idea.

## Demo data

- Realistic for the buyer: an aerospace buyer sees an aerospace plant, parts and test equipment. Use fictional names only, seeded before recording.
- The problem has to be big enough to matter on screen. "Ships 2 days late" works; a 7% overload on one day is too small.
- Record from a clean state: no developer chrome, toasts, stray hover chips or debug data.

## Voice

- Text-led by default. 92% of US mobile viewers watch muted (Verizon Media and Publicis Media, 2019), and LinkedIn and X autoplay muted.
- Add a voiceover version for YouTube or the website when the feature needs explaining. Use a professional human voice or top-tier text-to-speech. The on-screen words then shrink to headlines and labels.
